Trust

What you can hold us to

Ten commitments, written down so you can point at them later. Each one is either verifiable right now or something we will be judged on at handover.

Identity, not anonymity

quietmason is owner-operated: the person who scopes your work is the person who builds it. There is no sales layer, no subcontract chain, and one address for everything.

Money on published terms

You own the output

Code, data and credentials are yours from the first commit. We keep no license over your work, and we do not reuse your data, your catalog or your copy in another client's project.

Worth knowing: in the UK and the EU a freelancer keeps copyright unless the contract says otherwise, and plenty of contracts never say. Ours spells it out, in your favor, before work starts.

Minimum access, cleaned up after

We ask for the least access that lets the work happen: a staging copy over production, read-only over write, a sample export over your full database. Credentials stay out of source control.

at handover: access revoked or rotated on request, in writing

Claims you can reproduce

We do not guarantee rankings, traffic, revenue or that an AI assistant will mention you. When we quote a number, it comes from something you can open yourself: Search Console, a public URL, or the same input data.

if a number cannot be reproduced from your own account, treat it as marketing and ignore it

Client work stays unnamed by default

We do not name clients, show their screenshots or publish their metrics without written consent. That is why our work page describes systems rather than brands.

Right of reply, and corrections

If we ever write publicly about a company or a person (a supplier, a platform, a competitor), they get the substance of what we plan to say before publication, and a correction within five business days if we got it wrong. Anything we publish is reversible.

Work we refuse

We do not buy or sell links, write fake reviews or testimonials, guarantee outcomes, scrape behind logins or paywalls, or take work whose point is to misrepresent who you are or what you sell.

Basic hygiene, spelled out

Unique credentials per project, two-factor where we hold an account, secrets outside the repository, and no shared passwords across clients. If we make a mistake that exposes your data, you hear it from us first.

Complaints go somewhere

Email with "complaint" in the subject. You get an acknowledgment within one business day and a real answer, not a template. We do not retaliate, and we do not hold your data hostage over a disagreement.

Questions

Things clients ask before starting

Do you sign an NDA?

Yes, if you need one: send yours or we will return a plain mutual version. For most small projects an NDA is unnecessary because we will not name you or show your data anyway.

Who owns the code?

You do, from the first commit, including the repository history. If you want the work in your own Git host from day one, say so in the scope and we will build it there.

What if it does not work?

We do not invoice for something that does not do what the scope says. If a fixed-scope build cannot be delivered as written, you owe nothing for the undelivered part, and you keep whatever was already handed over. Changes of mind are not failures; those get a new scope.

Do you subcontract?

No. Work is done by the person you correspond with. If that ever has to change, you will be told before the project starts, not after.

How do payments work from outside your country?

Bank transfer or an agreed processor, invoiced per milestone in US dollars. Tell us at the start if you need a particular invoice format, purchase-order reference or tax wording.

Can we see work like ours?

We will walk you through a comparable system, the decisions behind it and the code, by email or on a call. What we will not do is invent a case study or publish a client's numbers to win a pitch.

Is data collection always acceptable?

We collect public data, respect rate limits and robots directives, and will decline work whose purpose is to break terms of service or the law. If a collection target is borderline, we say so before quoting rather than after.
