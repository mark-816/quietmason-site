What we actually do

The honest state of this field

AI search is early: the tooling is thin, the measurement is indirect, and results move slowly. That is why the work is worth doing now rather than in a year: the entities that get established first are the ones later described as the obvious answer. We prefer clients who want the foundation built properly over clients who want a graph pointing upward.

Questions

Common questions

  • Can you guarantee we get cited by an AI assistant? No, and nobody can. What we can do is make the facts about you present, consistent and readable, then measure what the engines actually return for your buyers' questions.
  • How do you measure it? A tracked prompt set: the questions your buyers type, re-run on a schedule across the answer engines, recording who gets named. It turns a vague worry into a number you can watch move.
  • What do you actually change? Entity details that agree everywhere, structured data that matches the visible page, and pages written as self-contained answers. We do not write about topics we know nothing about.
What the data says

Markup is not the mechanism

A 1,885-page study (Ahrefs, published 2026) tracked pages that added JSON-LD between August 2025 and March 2026 against 4,000 matched control pages: no measurable citation lift on any platform (Google AI Overviews, AI Mode or ChatGPT).

Structured data is still worth having: cited pages carry it far more often than uncited ones, and it removes ambiguity about who you are. But it is eligibility, not persuasion. The pages that get quoted answer the question in the first two sentences, in the buyer's own words, with a number or a date in them.

So what we actually spend the time on

  • Pages written as self-contained answers, not marketing prose
  • The exact questions your buyers type, re-run on a schedule
  • One set of facts about you, identical on every surface we can reach
  • Markup that matches the visible page, because a mismatch is a liability
