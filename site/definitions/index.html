Definitions

The words on this site, defined

Every line we sell, in one sentence you could repeat to a colleague, with what it costs and what it is not. If a term is missing, ask and we will add it.

In one line. A single automation workflow is one job that runs on a schedule ($250–1,000, 1–3 days); an enrichment pipeline keeps a dataset current ($700–4,600, 3–10 days); a technical SEO audit is a written list of what blocks crawling and ranking (from $400, 2–5 days); AEO or GEO is making the facts an assistant reads about you correct; the care plan is monitoring plus change hours from $90 a month.
Work

What the work is

Single automation workflow

One repeated job, moved off a person and onto a schedule: pull a feed into a sheet, file and resize incoming images, send a daily digest, keep two lists in step. It runs on a schedule, retries when a step fails and tells you when it breaks.

Price: $250–1,000 · 1–3 days · see the range

Not: a whole system. If two or more jobs depend on each other, that is a pipeline.

Data enrichment pipeline

A repeating flow that takes records you already have, finds what is missing, matches and cleans them, then writes back: validation, lookup, merge, conflict rules, write-back, and an audit log of what changed. Think of it as an assembly line for data.

Price: $700–4,600 · 3–10 days · see the range

Not: an analytics dashboard. It fixes records, it does not interpret them.

Multi-step integration

Connecting three to six tools you already pay for so data moves between them without anyone copying it: CRM to invoicing to support to reporting.

Price: $1,000–3,000 · 3–7 days · see the range

Not: a license resale. You keep paying your own vendors.

Fixed scope, fixed price

One written description of what will exist when the work is done, and one number for it. The number is agreed before anything starts, and the description is what we are measured against.

Price: half up front, half on handover; milestones above $3,000

Not: an hourly estimate. If we underestimated, that is our problem, not a new invoice.

Search

What the search terms mean

Technical SEO audit

A written account of what stops your pages being crawled, indexed or ranked: crawl report, index coverage from your own Search Console, redirect chains, canonicals, internal links, structured data, Core Web Vitals, each finding marked by impact, so you know what to do first.

Price: from $400 · 2–5 days · what it covers

Not: an ongoing retainer, and not content writing. It is the diagnosis, not the treatment.

AEO and GEO (AI search visibility)

Answer Engine Optimization and Generative Engine Optimization: the work of making sure the facts an assistant reads about you are present, correct and attributable, which means consistent entity details, markup that matches the visible page, and pages written as self-contained answers rather than marketing prose.

Price: scoped from $500 · what it covers

Not: a promise that a model recommends you. Nobody can promise that, and any page claiming it is selling you a feeling.

Tracked prompt set

The handful of questions your buyers actually type, written down once and then re-run against the answer engines on a schedule, recording who gets named and cited. It turns "are we visible in AI search" into a number you can watch.

Part of: the care plan's monthly report, or a one-off visibility check

Not: an audit of a model's secret algorithm. It records what came back, when.

llms.txt

A short markdown file at /llms.txt that tells AI crawlers what to read first: what the business does, the facts worth quoting, and the pages that answer a buyer's question. Ours lists prices and commitments, not every URL on the site.

Live example: quietmason.com/llms.txt

Not: a sitemap. A hundred links means you have published a sitemap and called it something else.

Terms that cost people money

  • Anti-bot pressure: how hard a source resists being read. It, not the data itself, is usually what moves a scraping price.
  • One-off versus monitored: a single collection is cheap; the same collection kept correct every week is a different job.
  • Silent failure: a scheduled job that reports success while doing nothing, usually because nobody configured failure detection. It is the most expensive kind of build.
  • Handover: the repository, the credentials and the runbook being yours, so the next person is not guessing.
